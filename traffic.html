How the model works

Rules both signals share

  • Arrivals. Vehicles arrive at random moments, at the average rate set by each road's count. Both signals get the identical list of arrivals.
  • One road at a time. Roads get green in the order N, E, S, W, then the cycle repeats every 120 seconds.
  • Leaving. On green, nothing moves for the first 2 seconds (drivers reacting), then one vehicle leaves every second. No vehicle leaves on amber or red.
  • Waiting time. For each vehicle: the second it leaves minus the second it arrived. Vehicles still queued at the end are counted too, with their wait so far.

The only difference

  • Fixed timer. Every road gets 27 seconds of green in every cycle, busy or not.
  • Smart signal. Before each cycle it works out each road's demand: vehicles left over after its last green, plus vehicles that arrived during the last cycle. It shares the 108 green seconds in proportion to demand.
  • Safety limits. No road gets less than 10 seconds or more than 60 seconds, so a quiet road is never ignored.
  • First cycle. The smart signal has seen no traffic yet, so it starts with the same 27 seconds each.

About the data and the limits

  • The starting numbers are simulated, not counted. They were chosen to look like an evening rush: the two MG Marg arms are the busiest, Vidhan Sabha Marg is medium and Ashok Marg is the quietest. They are not measurements of the real junction.
  • How they were made realistic. Vehicles arrive in uneven bunches (a Poisson pattern, the standard way to model random arrivals) instead of at perfectly even gaps, and the busiest road is set slightly above what a 27-second green can clear, which is what a jammed road means.
  • To use real data. Count each road for 15 minutes at the same time on 2 or 3 days, take the average, and type it in above with the day and time. Count only with a teacher or parent, from a safe place off the road, and do not photograph faces or number plates.
  • What the model leaves out. Turning vehicles, pedestrians, buses stopping, different vehicle sizes, and the signal ahead being blocked. Real results would differ; the comparison is fair because both signals face the same simplifications.
  • When smart does not help. Try the "Equal on all roads" pattern: there is nothing to rebalance, so both signals perform about the same.
